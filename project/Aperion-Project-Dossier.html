Imaging beyond what’s visible. Quantum-assisted ultrasound brain imaging.

One-sentence pitch: A team at Imperial College London showed a helmet of ultrasound sensors can image a stroke through the skull without radiation, but one image takes 32 hours of computing; we broke their reconstruction algorithm apart, found the step that is quantum-shaped (the wave simulation, over 95% of the compute), built and tested it as a Qiskit circuit, and show honestly what a quantum computer can and cannot do for it today.

Team: Anik Sahai (clinical & product, presenter lead) · Aaron Loh (math & numerics) · Charan Jarugula (quantum).

5How it works (high level)InnovationAnik

Guasch Fig. 1 (helmet geometry and 1,024-transducer prototype) + live 2D wave simulation through a real brain slice (MNI152 template) with a speed-of-sound key and a speed profile graph.

1,024 transducers, each a speaker and a microphone. One fires, all listen. Sound speeds up in skull (2,814 m/s) and slows in brain (~1,505 m/s grey matter, 1,552.5 white); blood is 1,578. Those speed changes are the image. Keep the hardware high level; the interesting part is the algorithm.

11Overview: guess, simulate, compare, repeat (your slide)Quantum RationaleAaron

1 Guess c_j · 2 Send q(t) · 3 Simulate p(x,t) (marked Quantum) · 4 Record d_pred · 5 Compare and update E = ½Σ(d_pred − d_meas)², c ← c − α∇E, ~80 rounds. Side card: step 3 and its adjoint are >95% of compute; wave equation → Schrödinger; 29 qubits could hold a head; readout is the hard part.

12How a computer simulates sound (derivation, 5 clicks)Quantum RationaleAaron
  1. Wave equation: (1/c²) ∂²p/∂t² = ∂²p/∂x² + q
  2. Grid: x_j = jh, t_n = nΔt
  3. Differences: ∂²p/∂x² ≈ (p_{j+1} − 2p_j + p_{j−1})/h²
  4. Update rule: p_j^{n+1} = 2p_j^n − p_j^{n−1} + (c_j²Δt²/h²)(p_{j+1}^n − 2p_j^n + p_{j−1}^n) + c_j²Δt² q_j^n
  5. Stability: c_max Δt/h ≤ 1 (1D), ≤ 1/√3 (3D); skull sets the clock
13How the image is found (derivation, 5 clicks)Quantum RationaleAaron
  1. Residual r = F(c) − d (predicted minus measured)
  2. Error E = ½‖r‖² (one number)
  3. Gradient ∇E = J_Fᵀ r, computed with one extra backward (“adjoint”) simulation
  4. Update c_{k+1} = Π[c_k − η(∇E + λ∇R)]
  5. Cost ≈ O(I·S·N_t·V) ≈ 80 × 100 × 2 × 2400 × 10⁸ ≈ 4×10¹⁵ updates (our estimate)
Forward = “what would I hear?” Misfit = “how wrong was I?” Adjoint = “play the error backwards to see where it came from.” E = 0 does not prove the anatomy is right (non-convex, local minima).
14Breaking their algorithm apartQuantum RationaleAaron

5-box flow: Guess (classical) → Forward sim (quantum candidate) → Misfit E (quantum candidate, one number) → Adjoint sim (quantum candidate) → Update (classical). >95% of compute in the two wave simulations; anything needing a full vector stays classical.

15Why quantum: sound becomes a Schrödinger equation (5 clicks)Quantum RationaleCharan
  1. Grid wave equation: d²φ/dt² = −B B† φ (B = which cells touch, weighted by speed)
  2. Stack ψ = (φ̇, −i B†φ): velocity and stretch
  3. i dψ/dt = Hψ, H = [[0, B],[B†, 0]], the equation a quantum computer runs
  4. ‖ψ‖² = wave energy, conserved = probability conserved
  5. ψ(t) = e^{−iHt}ψ(0) ≈ (e^{−iH_even Δt} e^{−iH_odd Δt})ⁿ ψ(0): Trotter steps
Plain English: the wave becomes a packet hopping along a chain of cells and links. n qubits hold 2ⁿ amplitudes, so 29 qubits could hold a whole head (~341M sites). But you can only read it out one sample at a time. Realistic gain is polynomial, at best quartic in 3D (Bösch 2025), not exponential. Costa’s H carries a 1/a factor, absorbed into B here.
16The 1D quantum algorithm we build on (Schade et al. 2024)Quantum RationaleCharan

Five steps: discretize the 1D elastic wave (varying ρ, μ) → rewrite as Schrödinger → load 2ⁿ grid points into n qubits → evolve with e^{−iHt} → measure. Their Qiskit circuit (state prep · time evolution · observable · measure). “64 grid points → 6 qubits; a head’s 341M sites → 29.”

17It runs on a real IBM quantum computer, at toy scaleQuantum RationaleCharan

Their figures: 64-point simulator matches the classical ODE solver; 9-point run on IBM Brisbane (blue) drifts from classical (black). Stats: 4–5 qubits · ~870 gates, no error mitigation · up to 60% error. Our addition: tissue speeds, Trotter steps, error mitigation, skull and bleed.

Note: Schade compiled the exact unitary (not Trotterized) and read out by state tomography at 1,000 shots. IBM Brisbane was retired in Nov 2025.

4. The ideas in plain English (for the script)

Ultrasound tomography / full-waveform inversion

  • The image is a list of sound speeds in ~10⁸ tiny cubes (0.5 mm). Sound is faster in skull, slower in brain, slightly faster in blood than brain (~5% vs grey matter, ~1.7% vs white).
  • FWI guesses that list, simulates the sound through it, compares predicted and measured recordings, and nudges the guess. Analogy: guessing what’s in a dark room by clapping and listening to echoes.
  • Each round needs a forward and an adjoint wave simulation for every transmitter: that is >95% of the compute and why it took 32 h.

Why the wave fits a quantum computer

  • Quantum computers only rotate a vector while keeping its length fixed. Lossless sound keeps total energy fixed. Build a vector whose length is the energy, and the wave becomes a quantum evolution.
  • Picture: a packet of amplitude hopping along a chain of cells and the links between them; hops are faster through skull. Our 7 cells + 8 links = 15 sites.
  • Two encodings: one qubit per site (15 qubits, cheap neighbour gates, 238 two-qubit gates, but only 15 numbers in play, so a laptop can do it) vs compact binary (4 qubits for 15 sites; n qubits hold 2ⁿ amplitudes, 29 for a head; but neighbours aren’t neighbouring bits, so 1,414 gates for 7 cells).
  • Readout wall: each shot reveals one site with probability = its energy share. Reading a full FWI round (~10⁹ numbers) would take ~10¹³ shots (~114 years at IBM’s rate). So ask for one number only (misfit, or bleed yes/no). Amplitude estimation could cut shots from 1/ε² to ~1/ε but needs deep circuits.
  • Time steps don’t shrink: sound still crosses ~440 cells, so quantum saves memory per step, not the number of steps.
