Where quantum fits

Step 3 and its adjoint are over 95% of the compute.

The wave equation can be rewritten as a Schrödinger equation, so a quantum circuit can run it directly.

A compact encoding could hold a whole head in 29 qubits.

Everything else stays classical. Reading the answer back out is the hard part.

Aaron. The whole method is one loop. One: guess the sound speed c_j in every voxel. Two: send in a known pulse q(t). Three: simulate the pressure p(x,t) through the guessed head. That is the quantum-shaped step, over 95% of the compute, because the wave equation can be written as a Schrödinger equation. Four: record the simulated pressure at the sensor positions. Five: compare with the real recordings to get the error E, then take a gradient descent step on c. Repeat about 80 rounds. Quantum replaces only the simulation; the gradient update stays classical, and reading results out of the quantum circuit is the real bottleneck.
