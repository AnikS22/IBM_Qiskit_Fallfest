Innovation & Creativity

What we built that’s new: turning a 1D research method into a brain-imaging quantum pipeline.

01

Brain tissue drives the gates

Skull, brain and blood sound speeds set each coupling gate’s strength, so the circuit carries a real head map.

Built on Schade 2024’s 1D elastic method

02

From 1D to 2D and 3D

We extended the 1D algorithm to 2D and a 3D grid of 27 cells on 135 qubits.

3D circuit error 0.0057 vs exact

03

A physics-aware noise filter

Our encoding allows exactly one excitation. Any shot with more is noise, so we throw it out.

IBM hardware: 0.013 → 0.081 (exact 0.104)

04

Two encodings, two jobs

15-qubit unary circuit for wave energy; compact 4-qubit circuit for signed pressure.

Twirling cut pressure error 78% on IBM Kingston

05

One loop, four engines

Same physics run through classical, gate-based, annealing and IBM hardware for a fair head-to-head.

SQA matched SA accuracy, ~10× slower

06

Designed around the readout wall

Ask the circuit for one number, the misfit, instead of the whole wave.

Hybrid one-number readout prototyped

Team repos team-quantum-ultrasound, IBM_Qiskit_Hackathon · IBM jobs on ibm_marrakesh and IBM Kingston

Six things that are ours. One: the tissue map sets the gate strengths. Two: we took a 1D paper to 2D and 3D. Three: our encoding has exactly one excitation, so any shot with more is noise and we drop it; that filter is what made the hardware result usable. Four: two circuits for two questions. Five: one loop with four interchangeable engines, so every comparison is fair. Six: we designed for the readout wall by asking for one number.
