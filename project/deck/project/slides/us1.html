Problem Definition & Relevance

How ultrasound screening of the brain works: a helmet of speakers and microphones.

WHY NOT A NORMAL ULTRASOUND?

Hospital ultrasound bounces high-frequency sound off tissue and reads the echo. The skull blocks and scrambles that, so it can’t see the brain. The helmet uses lower frequencies that pass through bone, and listens from every side.

1

Fire

One transducer sends a short pulse of sound into the head.

400 kHz peak · 3-cycle burst

2

Listen

Every other transducer records the pressure that reaches it, through skull and brain.

240 µs per recording

3

Repeat

Each of the 1,024 transducers takes a turn as the speaker.

>1 million recordings

No radiation, no magnet, small enough to travel. The catch: the recordings are not a picture yet.

The helmet is 1,024 small transducers, each one both a speaker and a microphone. One fires a short pulse; all the others record what arrives. Then the next one fires, and so on, until every transducer has had a turn: just over a million recordings. Normal ultrasound can’t do this because the skull blocks high frequencies; the helmet uses lower ones that pass through bone. The recordings on their own aren’t an image yet, and that is the next slide.
