A bleed is only ~2–5% faster than the brain around it: a tiny shift in arrival time.

Arrival times are the clue

Sound speeds up in skull and slows in brain. Each path through the head arrives a little early or late, and a bleed shifts the timing on every path that crosses it.

The whole wave, not just the echo

Full-waveform inversion uses the entire recorded wave to rebuild a 3D map of sound speed at 0.5 mm detail. That map is the brain image.

Turning a million recordings into that map is the step that takes ~32 hours.

Tissue sound speeds: Guasch et al. 2020 supplementary table · 0.5 mm grid: Methods

The image is a map of how fast sound travels. Water and brain are around 1,500 metres per second, skull is nearly twice that. Blood is only a few percent faster than brain, so a bleed shows up as a tiny change in arrival times across many paths. Full-waveform inversion uses the whole recorded wave to solve for the speed in every half-millimetre cube. That solve is the 32-hour step.
